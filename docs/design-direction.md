# Direction and content

## Three concepts

**01 — The Signal Atlas.** A warm, editorial research atlas: ivory paper, near-black ink and a single plum accent. Oversized sans-serif headlines meet italic serif phrases; an interactive architecture walkthrough connects real inputs, processing steps and outputs. Numbered chapters and original system diagrams turn complex ML projects into inspectable stories. Project outcomes and source links remain visible without interacting.

**02 — The Systems Terminal.** A restrained dark engineering workspace with fine grids, compact typographic hierarchies and an interactive agent-routing map. Project detail pages resemble concise technical field notes. It suits backend and orchestration work, though an entirely terminal-led visual identity risks hiding the breadth of research.

**03 — Beyond the Visible.** A spatial observatory using multispectral color bands, image-plane reveals and satellite-inspired compositions. It foregrounds computer vision and the ICPR publication. The visual metaphor is distinctive, but puts less emphasis on the equally strong RAG and backend projects.

## Selected: The Signal Atlas

The widest fit for a researcher who also ships applications. Technical diagramming connects retrieval, vision and infrastructure while editorial hierarchy makes the essentials accessible to recruiters. Motion follows meaning: signals connect, project previews respond, page changes gently reveal. A two-font system and lightweight SVG keep the effect portable to phones. Dark mode becomes charcoal paper with pale ink and a muted pink accent.

## Hero interaction: inside the systems

The original torus represented a connected information space: signals becoming insight. It was a visual metaphor, with an arbitrary radius slider that did not correspond to a project parameter. The revised hero keeps the editorial atlas but makes its signature interaction explain the actual work.

Choose Retrieval, Vision or Agents, then inspect one of four numbered steps. A selected node reveals its tool, input, output and purpose. GSSTB Scholar explains question rewriting, hybrid retrieval, grounding and cited answers; ShelfMind explains detection, catalog matching and planogram comparison; CodeResidency explains intent routing, specialist responsibilities and contextual feedback. These are simplified architecture walkthroughs from the same repository descriptions used in the case studies, not live model executions or generated benchmark results. All copy lives in the typed `portfolio.hero.systems` data.

This makes the hero specific to Kush's systems and lets a recruiter understand the engineering before opening a full case study. Native buttons, pressed states, visible focus, a polite live region and a stable detail area support keyboard and mobile use. No pointer-only interaction, arbitrary tuning controls or continuous animation is required.
